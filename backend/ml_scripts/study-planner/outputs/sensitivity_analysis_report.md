# Sensitivity Analysis — Hybrid Priority Layer & Exam-Prep Model

Generated by `ml_scripts/study-planner/sensitivity_analysis.py`. Raw data: `sensitivity_analysis_report.csv` (51 rows — 5 constant groups × 3-5 tested values each × 3 fixed scenarios). **No production code was changed** — this is analysis only.

## Metrics (evaluateSchedule)

- **pct_high_priority_scheduled_before_deadline** — fraction of final-priority-High tasks that received their full `estimated_hours_needed` before their deadline (not in `overload_warning`). `n/a` when a scenario has zero High-priority tasks under the tested config, rather than a misleading 0/1.
- **avg_lead_time_high_priority** — for fully-scheduled High tasks, average days between their *last* scheduled session and their deadline. Larger = more buffer. Direction assumed "larger is better" (more slack before a high-stakes deadline); not universally true (a student could also read a huge lead time as "front-loaded, then idle") but it's the more defensible default reading.
- **daily_load_variance** — population variance of total scheduled hours across the 7 days. Reported without an assumed "better" direction, as instructed — low variance means evenly spread load (arguably gentler pacing), but a legitimately front-loaded plan (get it out of the way early) would show high variance and could still be a "better" outcome for some students. No claim either way.
- **overload_total_hours** — total shortfall hours across all `overload_warning` entries. Lower = more of the estimated need was actually met.

## Scenarios (fixed, reused identically across every sweep)

| Scenario | Free time | Tasks | Exams | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **light** | 21h/week | 2 assignments | 1 (25d out, strong performer) | Generous capacity relative to demand |
| **typical** | 21h/week | 4 assignments (incl. one >30d hard-floor case, two deliberately ML-disagrees-with-deadline cases) | 2 (8d/14d out — matches real sample data) | Realistic, moderately tight |
| **heavy** | 14h/week | 4 assignments | 4 (8/10/12/14d out — matches real 4-exam sample) | Deliberately oversubscribed |

## Headline finding: the two extreme scenarios are largely uninformative

**`light`** has so much spare capacity (21h supply vs. ~9h demand) that almost nothing binds — every sweep produces identical or near-identical results (`pct_high=1.0`, `lead_time=3`, `overload=0` in all but one cell). **`heavy`** is the opposite extreme: total demand so far exceeds its 14h/week supply that changing any single constant within a reasonable range never relieves the crunch — `pct_high=0.0` and `overload_h` stays in the 17.5-34.75h range across every single tested value of every constant. This is itself a legitimate finding, not a null result: **the system's constants only matter in the realistic-but-tight middle ground** (`typical`); at the extremes, outcomes are dominated by whether total demand structurally exceeds/undercuts total supply, not by exactly where a threshold sits. All substantive conclusions below come from `typical`.

## Per-constant findings

### 1. Base tier day-thresholds — **moderately sensitive, but current sits on a stable plateau**

`typical` lead time: -5d → **1.0** | -2d → **5.5** | **0 (current) → 5.5** | +2d → **3.333** | +5d → **3.333**.

Current (0) and -2 give *identical* results — a small perturbation in either direction near current doesn't move the outcome, which is the signature of a stable region, not a knife-edge choice. But a *larger* shift in either direction (-5, or +2/+5) does change which tasks land in the High tier and measurably changes average lead time (drops by ~1.6-4.5 days). `overload_total_hours` was completely unaffected by this sweep (stayed at 6.75h throughout) — reordering priority within a fixed total demand/supply gap doesn't change how much total work goes unmet, only which task gets buffer. **Verdict: current thresholds are reasonable and not fragile at realistic step sizes, but the system is not robust to a large threshold change (±5 days) — worth stating as a real, bounded limitation rather than claiming total insensitivity.**

### 2. ML modifier clamp — **current value already captures nearly all the benefit; low sensitivity above it**

`typical` lead time: clamp=0 (pure rule) → **1.0** | clamp=1 (current) → **5.5** | clamp=2 → **5.5**. `light` also flipped from 1 High-priority task at clamp≥1 to **zero** High-priority tasks at clamp=0 (`pct_high` correctly reports `n/a`, not a misleading 0).

Turning ML influence off entirely (clamp=0) is the one case that visibly hurts outcomes (lead time collapses from 5.5 to 1.0 days) — confirming the hybrid layer's ML modifier is doing real, positive work, not just cosmetic. But going from the current ±1 to ±2 produced **zero additional change** in any scenario/metric. **Verdict: clamp=1 (current) is a well-justified choice — it already captures the measurable benefit of allowing ML influence; there is no evidence in this data that allowing a full 2-tier ML swing would improve outcomes further, so the current, more conservative value (which also better protects the "deadline stays dominant" design intent from Section 5d) is preferable to clamp=2, not just equally good.**

### 3. Exam prep total budget hours — **overload scales roughly linearly with budget, as expected; not "surprising" sensitivity**

`typical` overload: 8h → **1.5h** | 12h (current) → **6.75h** | 16h → **11.75h**. `heavy`: 17.5h → 26.25h → 34.75h (also roughly linear).

This is a direct, mechanical consequence of asking for more total hours against fixed free-time supply — not evidence of fragility, just the honest cost side of a thoroughness/feasibility trade-off inherent to the constant's meaning. **Verdict: no single value is "correct" from this data alone — 12h is a defensible middle point (8h risks under-preparing, 16h meaningfully increases realistic overload risk in a typical week). Recommend keeping 12h.**

### 4. Exam prep curve shape — **low sensitivity; current back-loaded curve is a safe, defensible choice**

`typical` overload: flatter (25/40/35) → **6.25h** | current (15/35/50) → **6.75h** | front-loaded (30/40/30) → **5.5h**. A ~1.25h swing on ~24h of total exam demand (≈5% relative movement) — small.

The front-loaded curve edges out the others on `overload_total_hours` in this data, but the margin is modest and the qualitative rationale for a back-loaded curve (light familiarization early, heavy concentrated review just before the exam — see Section 8a) is a genuine pedagogical judgment this analysis doesn't evaluate (none of the 4 metrics measure retention/learning quality, only schedule feasibility). **Verdict: the system is not meaningfully sensitive to curve shape on feasibility grounds — current curve is defensible; the front-loaded alternative is a legitimate, only slightly better-scoring alternative worth keeping in mind, not a clear replacement.**

### 5. Performance multiplier range — **overload scales with how aggressive the "weak" multiplier is; current is a reasonable middle**

`typical` overload: narrower (1.2/1.0/0.9) → **5.0h** | current (1.4/1.0/0.75) → **6.75h** | wider (1.6/1.0/0.6) → **8.25h**.

Directly proportional to how much extra time a struggling module's exam-prep is boosted — again a real, expected trade-off (more support for weak performance costs more schedulable time), not a fragile/surprising swing. **Verdict: current range is a defensible middle point between under-supporting struggling students (narrower) and risking system-wide overload during a heavy exam season (wider). Recommend keeping current.**

## Overall Recommendation

**(a) Confirm the current production constants as reasonable and defensible**, with one caveat flagged honestly:

- Clamp (±1) and curve shape: low sensitivity, current values solidly justified by this data.
- Exam budget (12h) and performance multiplier range (1.4/1.0/0.75): moderate, *expected* sensitivity (both are direct trade-off dials, not fragile edge cases) — current values sit at reasonable middle points, no better value is evident from this data alone (would require actual learning-outcome data, not just schedule-feasibility metrics, to optimize further).
- Base tier thresholds: the one constant with genuine, somewhat non-obvious sensitivity — stable under small perturbation (±2 days) but not under a larger one (±5 days). Not a reason to change the current values, but worth documenting as a real, bounded limitation rather than claiming full robustness.

No revised values are recommended. No production code changed.

## Limitations of this analysis

- 3 fixed scenarios, not a distribution — results characterize behavior at these specific load points, not a full response surface.
- `daily_load_variance` was near-zero or exactly zero in `typical`/`heavy` across every sweep (both scenarios' scarce free time gets fully consumed in a tightly packed way regardless of constants) — this metric only showed real variation in the `light` scenario, where it's least meaningful (nothing was actually constrained). A future run with an intermediate-capacity scenario (between `light` and `typical`) might make this metric more informative.
- Raw ML labels for assignments were fixed, hand-chosen scenario inputs (not real model predictions) specifically to create some ML/deadline disagreement worth testing the clamp against — documented in `sensitivity_analysis.py`, not hidden.
